Audio & Signal Processing2026Research · UIUC

Learning Personalized HRTFs from Binaural Recordings

Question
Can personalised 3D audio be learned from a cheap pair of microphones, as a first step towards hearing aids tuned to each person's ears?
Method
Recorded sound at both ears from marked angles and estimated head-related transfer functions two ways: broadband least squares and per-frequency cross-correlation.
Finding
Listeners placed the synthesised sound within about 10° (±5°) of its true direction at most angles.
Role
Student researcher, SINRG, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, with Prof. Romit Roy Choudhury.

AstrophysicsSummer 2026Research · SSP

Orbit Determination of Near-Earth Asteroid 2015 GF27

Question
Where is near-Earth asteroid 2015 GF27 headed, working only from a few telescope observations?
Method
From three observations taken at the LCO McDonald Observatory, Texas, I used the Method of Gauss to compute the asteroid's position and velocity and derive its six classical orbital elements.
Finding
Our team predicted the preliminary orbit, with uncertainty estimated by Monte Carlo simulation, and presented it at the University of Wisconsin–River Falls.
Program
Summer Science Program, one of the most selective in the US (about 5% acceptance). Mentors: Dr. Truong Le, Dr. Marta McNeese, Dr. Michael Rodruck.

Medical AI2026Research · Oral

Diagnostic-Language Dependence and Robustness in Multimodal Medical LLMs

Question
When AI models diagnose chest X-rays, are they reading the image, or the words in the report next to it?
Method
Evaluated five multimodal models on 657 CheXpert Plus cases, removing diagnostic terms from reports and testing 29 controlled perturbations.
Finding
With diagnostic terms removed, accuracy fell to 16% even with the image present. Demographic changes caused the largest spurious flips.
Venue
TrustFMI workshop at ACCV 2026, Springer LNCS. Oral presentation in Osaka, Japan.

Speech AIGrade 12Project · Demo

Clear Voice: speech recognition for cerebral palsy

Question
A standard recogniser turned "My leg is hurting" into "Why do you hate me?" Could AI understand a young woman with dysarthria well enough to replace an exam scribe?
Method
Wrote 149 everyday sentences around her hardest words, recorded 16.7 minutes at home, and fine-tuned Whisper to her voice with five-fold cross-validation.
Finding
Word errors fell from 30.0% to 6.6%, and to 5.9% on a later day. 78% of sentences now come out word-perfect.
Partner
Spastics Society of Karnataka. Next: a framework so parents can record data at home for other students.

Assistive RoboticsGrade 12Project · Demo

Voice-controlled semi-autonomous wheelchair

Question
When my grandfather lost the ability to walk, he could only move when someone was free to push him. Could an ordinary wheelchair drive itself to a room when you say its name?
Method
Added two 250 W geared motors with Arduino control, and a Raspberry Pi 5 that interprets room names with a language model and plans routes on a floor-plan graph by dead reckoning.
Finding
Holds 0.31 m/s with 0.6% variation and predicts distance within 1% over 9.2 m. Built for ₹48,500 and in supervised daily use.
Role
Designed and built it, with guidance from Prof. Ratna Ghosh, Jadavpur University. Submitted to IRIS.

Medical AIGrade 11 – nowProject · Demo soon

Phone-based AI cataract screening

Question
At our eye camps, hundreds of villagers queue for a few doctors. Could a phone screen for cataract without a slit-lamp microscope?
Method
Collected 300+ images at the partner hospital, designed an imaging set-up from a 25D lens, an LED and a smartphone, and trained a CNN on dilated eyes.
Finding
Detects Grade 2 and Grade 3 cataract with about 90% accuracy. Camp doctors approved it, and I trained village volunteers to use it.
Next
The hospital has a roadmap to deploy it as kiosks at stations and village centres.

The Lens Project

Founder · 2021 – now · Free eye care for elderly villagers and leprosy survivors in rural West Bengal
Diana Award, 2026

My grandparents, both doctors, served their village community for decades. On visits I saw elders lose their livelihoods to failing eyesight, and leprosy survivors turned away by stigma. When age brought my grandparents' service to a close, I started The Lens Project in Grade 8 to carry it on.

I built a team of doctors, village youth and village councillors to run eye camps twice a year, and I raise the funds for cataract surgery, lenses, medicines and post-operative care, delivered with Vivekananda Mission Ashram Eye Hospital, Midnapur. In the early years villagers doubted we were apolitical. Repeated visits and honest conversations won their trust.

Turnout has grown from about 450 patients per camp in 2023 to about 700 in 2025. When I leave for college in 2027, a core group of 15 village youth will run the camps on the ground while I manage fundraising and logistics online and return for at least one camp a year.
